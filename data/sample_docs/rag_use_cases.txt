Common Use Cases for RAG

Internal document assistants let employees ask questions in plain language
about company wikis, policy documents, and internal knowledge bases,
returning answers grounded in the actual current documents instead of
relying on someone remembering where the right page is.

Customer support assistants use RAG to answer questions about a product by
retrieving from help center articles, documentation, and past support
tickets, so answers stay consistent with published material and update
automatically whenever the underlying documentation changes, without
needing to retrain any model.

Code search and developer assistants apply the same retrieval pattern to a
codebase: source files are chunked, often by function or class rather than
by a fixed character count, embedded, and retrieved to help answer
questions about how a particular piece of functionality works or where it
is implemented.

Legal and compliance review tools retrieve relevant clauses or precedents
from large document collections such as contracts or regulations, where
faithfulness matters especially heavily, since an ungrounded or hallucinated
answer in this domain can carry real legal or financial consequences.

Research and literature assistants retrieve from collections of papers or
articles to help summarize findings or answer questions across a body of
work too large for a person to read in full, with citations back to the
specific source material.

Across all of these use cases, the same underlying pipeline applies:
ingest and chunk the domain's documents, embed and index them, retrieve the
most relevant pieces for a given question, and generate a grounded answer.
What changes between use cases is mostly the source material, the chunking
strategy suited to that material's structure, and how strict the grounding
and faithfulness requirements need to be for that domain.
