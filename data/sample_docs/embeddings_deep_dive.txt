Embeddings in Depth

An embedding is a fixed-length vector of numbers produced by a machine
learning model to represent the meaning of a piece of text. Two pieces of
text with similar meaning are mapped to vectors that are close together in
the embedding space, even if they share few or no words in common.

Embedding models are trained on large amounts of text using objectives that
pull semantically related pairs of text closer together and push unrelated
pairs further apart. The result is a space where distance corresponds to
meaning rather than surface-level word overlap.

Dimensionality is an important property of an embedding model. A model like
sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 produces 384-dimensional vectors,
which is considered compact. Larger models can produce 768, 1024, or even
3072-dimensional vectors. Higher dimensionality can capture more nuance but
costs more memory to store and more compute to compare.

Embedding models fall into two broad categories: general-purpose models
trained on broad web text, and domain-specific models fine-tuned on
specialized text such as legal documents, medical literature, or source
code. A domain-specific embedding model often produces noticeably better
retrieval quality within its domain than a general-purpose one, because it
has learned which distinctions actually matter in that domain.

A critical operational rule: the embedding model used to index documents
and the embedding model used to embed queries at search time must be the
same model. Different embedding models produce vectors in unrelated spaces,
so comparing a query vector from one model against document vectors from a
different model produces meaningless results, even if both models are
otherwise high quality.
